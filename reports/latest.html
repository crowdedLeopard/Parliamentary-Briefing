Government expects Microsoft to share lessons from the Microsoft 365 outage

Adam Dance (LD, Yeovil) to DCMS; answered by Ian Murray · Answered 23 Nov (UIN 43125)

Asked what assessment had been made of the cost to the UK economy of the 12–13 November Microsoft 365 outage, the minister said no assessment has been made because the impacts are still emerging. The Government expects Microsoft to share its lessons learned and will engage further on preventing such incidents and on faster remediation. He noted that cloud services are regulated under the NIS Regulations 2018 and that the Government expects a report to have been lodged with the regulator, the Information Commissioner.

Why it matters. Microsoft is named on the record as a resilience risk, with an explicit ministerial expectation of follow-up. It lands the day MPs debate Lords amendments to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, which updates the same NIS regime. Expect further questions; a proactive lessons-learned line to DCMS and DSIT would get ahead of them.

Lords Science and Technology Committee · Launched 23 Nov; evidence by 23:59, Friday 15 January 2027

The inquiry will examine the UK's compute and data-centre strategy and whether the UK can capture value in an AI economy dominated by US firms and overseas chip manufacturing. Why it matters. This goes straight to Microsoft's core issues: hyperscale investment, grid and planning constraints, sovereignty and US dominance. A Microsoft submission, and possibly a witness slot, would put the company's numbers and position on the record before the committee frames the debate.
