Question and answer

To ask His Majesty's Government what steps they are taking to reduce any risks attached to the NHS's reliance on systems provided by Microsoft.

The NHS uses Microsoft services alongside technology from a range of other suppliers. Reliance on any major technology provider creates potential operational, cyber, and supply-chain risks, and NHS England manages these through technical resilience, business continuity, cyber security, supplier management, and incident-response arrangements, as well as considering these issues when selecting and designing services. NHS England maintains 24/7 arrangements to detect, manage, and escalate significant digital incidents across its full technology estate, where disruption could have an operational or patient impact. To ask the Attorney General, what assessment she has made of the potential impact of the time taken for digital evidence analysis on the time taken for the Crown Prosecution Service to make charging decisions in rape cases.

Delivering timely justice for victims of rape and serious sexual assault is a priority for this government. The Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) is working closely with the police and other criminal justice partners to ensure that access to justice. The increase in scale and scope of digital technologies is creating new avenues for abuse and exploitation. As a result, the volume of digital evidence that is required for a case to be investigated and a charging decision to be made has increased significantly, placing increased pressure on criminal justice agencies. A key component of the CPS' National Operating Model (NOM), launched in July 2023, is strengthened collaboration with policing services and an emphasis on improved early interactions at the start of a case. This includes an offer of early advice, enabling the police to seek advice from a CPS prosecutor at a much earlier point in the case, and a meeting at pre-charge stage. These early discussion between police and prosecutors can help identify which electronic devices, accounts, time periods, and types of data are relevant to the case reducing the amount of time taken to review digital evidence. Since the launch of the CPS' NOM, the median number of days between a case being referred and a decision to charge fell from 31 days in 2022/23, to 6 days in 2025/26. This indicates that, despite the increased demands regarding digital evidence, the CPS' efforts to improve collaboration with policing has resulted in overall improvements in timeliness in adult rape-flagged cases.
