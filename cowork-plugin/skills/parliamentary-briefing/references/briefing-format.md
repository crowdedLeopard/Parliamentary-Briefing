# Briefing format

Email the briefing as formatted HTML: headings, short paragraphs and bulleted items. Keep the styling simple so it renders in Outlook. Don't attach files.

## Subject

`Parliamentary briefing – [date(s) covered] – [top headline]`

Examples:
- `Parliamentary briefing – 23 November 2026 – Lords launches inquiry into UK compute`
- `Parliamentary briefing – 20–22 November 2026 – MPs press Cabinet Office on cloud dependency`

## Opening

One or two sentences giving the dates covered and the single most important development.

## Sections, in this order

1. **Very important items for immediate attention**
   Items as defined in topics-and-lens.md. Leave the section out if there are none.
2. **Parliament Today**
   A brief summary of today's relevant debates, departmental questions, bill stages, committee sessions, publications and events. Give each item the time, the House or venue, the lead member or department, one or two lines on why it matters to Microsoft, and a link. If nothing relevant is scheduled, say so in one line.
3. **Issues arising in debates**
   Chamber, Westminster Hall, Grand Committee and Public Bill Committee contributions.
4. **Questions**
   Oral questions first, then written answers, then notable newly tabled questions. Group a member's series of related tabled questions into one item.
5. **Committees**
   Inquiries launched (with evidence deadlines), evidence sessions held, reports and government responses published, and notable correspondence.
6. **Other sources**
   Written ministerial statements, and Commons Library, Lords Library and POST publications.

Leave out sections 3 to 6 if they have nothing relevant. Don't pad them.

## Item layout

**[Short headline]**: [Member name] ([Party], [constituency or peerage if useful]) / [Department or committee], [date].

Then one to four sentences on what happened and why it matters to Microsoft. The more relevant the item, the more detail it gets.

End with a link to the specific page: the debate, question, statement, committee page or publication.

For written answers, give the minister's answer in brief. For holding answers or questions awaiting an answer, say so.

## Coverage note (always last)

A short paragraph covering:
- the dates covered, and whether Parliament sat on each;
- what was screened: the number of written questions screened from the pre-fetched file (`questionsScanned`), or that the file was unavailable and written-question coverage is partial;
- which committees were checked;
- any source that could not be read.

## When nothing relevant was found

Send a short email saying so, with the Parliament Today section and the coverage note.
