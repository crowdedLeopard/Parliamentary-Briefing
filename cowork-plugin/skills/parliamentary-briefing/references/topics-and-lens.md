# Topics and reader's lens

## Screen for

- Microsoft, Xbox and LinkedIn
- Competitors, especially Google, AWS/Amazon, OpenAI and Anthropic
- Cloud computing, and government dependency on, or exit from, cloud and IT suppliers
- AI, AI safety and AI regulation, including any AI Bill
- Compute capacity, sovereign AI and AI infrastructure
- Online and digital safety, including online scams and fraud
- Data centres, including planning, energy, water and AI Growth Zones
- Text and data mining, and AI and copyright
- Cyber security, including the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill
- Digital skills
- Tech and digital sovereignty
- Public-sector AI and procurement

## Reader

A senior government affairs lead at Microsoft UK; the scheduled task's prompt names them.

Their lens:
- the UK Government (No 10, DSIT, HMT), Parliament, industry bodies and think tanks;
- positioning against Google and AWS;
- opposition to energy use and data-centre growth, and the arguments around digital sovereignty, Big Tech dominance and putting AI safety first.

Skip: consumer gadgets, developer tools, crypto, stock moves and US-only politics.

## Weighting

The most important items, which go in section 1, are:
1. anything naming Microsoft or its products;
2. questions about dependency on Microsoft, AWS or other IT suppliers;
3. regulatory moves on core issues;
4. new committee inquiries or calls for evidence on core issues, with the deadline;
5. major opposition to data centres.

After that, rank items by how directly they bear on Microsoft's position. Give these more weight:
- government ministers' answers that state policy, figures or timetables;
- cross-party pressure, such as several members on the same issue;
- anything that feeds the sovereignty or Big Tech dominance arguments.

A single routine question with a holding answer gets one line.
